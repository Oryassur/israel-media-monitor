Methodology. Every hour, the pipeline captures the headlines on each outlet's homepage. Each headline gets a prominence weight reflecting how top-heavy reader attention is: the lead story ×10, slots 2–5 ×5, 6–10 ×3, 11–20 ×1. Attention is the Israel-related share of the top-20 window's total weight, averaged equally across the selected outlets (only the top-20 window is ingested as of 2026-09-09; stories below it are counted for parser health but not tracked). Sentiment scores each Israel-related headline's framing toward Israel on a fixed rubric (−2 hostile … +2 sympathetic) using a language model; scores are cached per headline and record the model + rubric version. Hours where an outlet failed to respond are excluded, not counted as zero. The chart is a diverging stacked bar: bar height is the attention share, its colored segments split that attention by headline sentiment (red hostile, blue sympathetic), and the neutral band — including still-unscored headlines in fainter gray — straddles the zero baseline. For runs before the composition columns existed (the ≈ date in the legend) the split is approximated from the headline archive. Headlines are grouped into cross-outlet stories by a language model (clustering model + version recorded per assignment; story titles are model-written). Political lean (Left / Center / Right) is assigned mechanically from published ratings — AllSides Media Bias Ratings where the outlet is rated (Lean Left/Left → Left, Center → Center, Lean Right/Right → Right), Ad Fontes Media for international outlets they cover, and the widely documented characterization otherwise; the per-outlet basis is noted in config/sources.yaml. Headline drill-down covers the most recent 7 days. On 2026-09-09 the panel expanded from 20 to 30 outlets; Associated Press and El País were retired at the same time (persistent fetch failures) — their history remains in the daily series.
